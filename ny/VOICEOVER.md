## A. Final script
Down on a stock this year? Sell by December thirtieth, and that loss can cut the tax on your gains.
A capital loss offsets capital gains. Only gains, not your salary.
Twelve thousand in gains, and another stock down eight thousand? Sell the loser, and your net gain is four thousand. Half is taxable: two thousand, not six.
Why the thirtieth? Trades settle one business day later, and settlement sets the tax year.
The trap: buy it back within thirty days, before or after, or let your spouse buy it, and the loss is denied for this year.
It usually moves into the cost of the new shares instead.
Losses inside a TFSA or RRSP? You can't claim them at all.
Check your gains before the thirtieth. Your broker can confirm the cut-off.
Next: the CRA form for investments outside Canada. Follow so you don't miss it.
