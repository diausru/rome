Description:
Down on a stock this year? In Canada, a capital loss can offset your capital gains, but only if the sale lands in the right year.

The rule: capital losses offset capital gains only (not your salary or other income).
Example: a $12,000 gain on one stock and an $8,000 loss on another. Sell the loser and your net gain is $4,000; half is taxable, so $2,000 instead of $6,000.

Why December 30 (2026): Canadian trades settle one business day later (T+1), and CRA treats the settlement date as the date you sold. A trade on Wednesday, December 30 settles December 31; a December 31 trade settles in 2027. Confirm the cut-off with your broker.

The trap: if you or an affiliated person (like your spouse) buys the same stock within 30 days before or after the sale and still holds it 30 days later, it's a superficial loss and the loss is denied. It usually gets added to the cost of the shares bought back instead.

Losses inside a TFSA or RRSP can't be claimed at all.
