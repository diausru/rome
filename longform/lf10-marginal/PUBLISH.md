## Description
Three coworkers in Ontario get the same $1,000 raise.

| Salary | Kept from the raise |
|---|---|
| $65K | $643 |
| $90K | $704 |
| $120K | $566 |

Why does the middle earner keep the most? Because your real marginal rate isn't just your tax bracket. It's everything that comes off the next dollar: federal tax, Ontario tax, the Ontario surtax, the health premium, CPP and EI.

We draw that line from $45K to $270K with 2026 rates and show:
• the steps where each bracket starts
• the "valley" between $68,900 and $85,000, where EI, CPP and CPP2 stop for the year (and why your last paycheques of the year can be bigger)
• the surtax, the federal basic personal amount shrinking above $181,440, and the health-premium bumps
• the RRSP twist: a deduction saves income tax only, so a $1,000 RRSP saves $296 at both $65K and $90K, and $434 at $120K
• carry-forward: deducting the same $1,000 at $120K instead of $50K saves about $434 instead of $207

The examples assume a single employee with no family benefits. Income-tested benefits (CCB, GST credit) can raise your real rate. General information, not advice. Sources: canada.ca, CRA T4127 (2026).
