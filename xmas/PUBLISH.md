Description:
Giving money or investments this Christmas? Canada has no gift tax, and a gift you receive isn't income. But some gifts can still cost the person who gives them.

1) Money you give your spouse or common-law partner to invest: the interest, dividends and capital gains are taxed to you (the attribution rules). Exception: money your spouse puts into their own TFSA isn't attributed back to you.

2) Money you give kids under 18 (children, grandchildren, other related minors): interest and dividends are taxed to you.

3) Gifts that aren't cash (shares, a cottage, crypto) to anyone but your spouse: CRA treats it as sold at fair market value.
Example: shares bought for $10,000 and worth $30,000, given to your adult son → you report a $20,000 capital gain; half of it, $10,000, is added to your income. His cost becomes $30,000.

4) Gifts from your employer: non-cash gifts and awards up to $500 a year in total are tax-free (only the excess is taxable). Cash and near-cash gifts are always taxable.

The gift itself is tax-free. What it earns, and what grew before you gave it, may not be. Exceptions exist (prescribed-rate loans, principal residence, separation); ask a professional before a large gift.
