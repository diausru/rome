## 1. Research record (REAL TAX INFORMATION MODE)
canada.ca is egress-blocked here; claims read from canada.ca search-result text (restricted to canada.ca, 2026-10-06).

| # | Claim used | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | As a rule, you can deduct any reasonable current expense you incur to earn (business) income; personal expenses aren't deductible, so only the business part of an expense is claimed | CRA Guide T4002, Chapter 3 (…/publications/t4002/t4002-5.html) |
| R2 | Capital expenses (a lasting benefit) can't be deducted in full when bought; capital property is claimed over time through capital cost allowance (CCA) | T4002 Ch. 3; CRA "Claiming capital cost allowance (CCA)" |
| R3 | Food, beverages and entertainment: generally a maximum of 50% of the lesser of the amount incurred and a reasonable amount; exceptions exist (e.g., billed to the client, all-employee events, charity fundraisers) | CRA "Line 8523 – Meals and entertainment (allowable part only)" |
| R4 | Employees can deduct employment expenses only if the contract required them to pay the expenses (and no non-taxable allowance covered them); the employer completes Form T2200 | CRA Guide T4044 "Employment Expenses"; line 22900 |
| R5 | Keep records to support income and expenses, generally for a minimum of six years from the end of the last tax year they relate to | CRA "Business records"; "Where to keep your records, for how long…" |

### Example (ILLUSTRATION, labelled "EXAMPLE")
A $1,200 annual phone bill with 60% business use → $720 claimed (the business part, R1). The business-use percentage must be reasonable and supportable; it is not a CRA figure.

### Exceptions and dependencies
- The 50% meals limit has exceptions (R3) → "generally".
- Employees vs self-employed (R4) → separate beat.
- The CCA class and rate depend on the asset → not detailed.
- What's "reasonable" depends on the facts → general info, not advice.
