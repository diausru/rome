Description:
Take CPP at 60, or wait until 70? The difference is permanent.

Start before 65 and your CPP retirement pension is reduced by 0.6% for each month, up to 36% at 60. Wait past 65 and it grows 0.7% a month, up to 42% at 70. Example: if your pension would be $1,000 a month at 65, it's $640 at 60 and $1,420 at 70, more than double from 60 to 70.

Simplified break-even (ignoring inflation, tax and returns): waiting from 65 to 70 pays off if you live past about 82. The federal Retirement Hub notes about half of today's retirees will reach 90. Health matters: with serious health concerns, earlier may make sense; in good health, later may pay more.

It's a lifetime decision. Run your own numbers (My Service Canada Account shows your estimate) and get advice.

Sources: Service Canada, CPP: When to start your pension; Retirement Hub (canada.ca). $1,000 is an example. General information, not financial advice.
