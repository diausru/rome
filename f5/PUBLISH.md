Description:
Selling your home in Canada? The gain is usually tax-free thanks to the principal residence exemption. Usually.

Example: bought for $300,000, lived there 5 years, sold for $450,000 → a $150,000 gain that can be fully exempt.

Three ways it goes wrong:
1. You still have to report the sale and designate the home (Schedule 3 + Form T2091). A late designation can cost $100 a month, up to $8,000.
2. One home per family unit, per year. Own a cottage too? Only one is covered for any given year.
3. The flipping rule (sales since 2023): sell a home you owned less than 365 days and the profit is fully taxed as business income, with no exemption, unless a life event applies (for example a death in the family or a new child joining the household).

So it's tax-free, if you report it and it was really your home.

Sources: CRA (canada.ca): principal residence, Folio S1-F3-C2, flipped property rules. The $300,000 home is an example. General information, not advice.
