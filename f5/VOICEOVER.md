## A. Final script
Selling your home in Canada? The gain is usually tax-free. Usually.
Here are three ways it goes wrong.
Say you bought for three hundred thousand, lived there five years, and sold for four fifty. That hundred-fifty-thousand gain can be fully exempt.
One: you still have to report the sale and designate the home on your return. Designate late, and the penalty can reach eight thousand dollars.
Two: one home per family, per year. Own a cottage too? Only one of them is covered for any given year.
Three: the flipping rule. Sell a home you owned less than three hundred sixty-five days, and the profit is fully taxed as business income.
Unless a life event applies, like a death in the family or a new baby.
So it's tax-free, if you report it, and if it was really your home.
Next: renting out a property, and the deduction that can backfire. Follow so you don't miss it.
