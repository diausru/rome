Description:
Your T2202 tuition slip is worth real money at tax time. The question is: whose money?

The rule (federal, 2026): the tuition credit is 14% of eligible tuition shown on your T2202, if you paid that school more than $100 in the year.
Example: $8,000 of tuition = a $1,120 federal credit.

Step 1: the student claims it first. It must bring the student's own tax to zero. With $12,000 from a summer job, a student already owes no federal tax, so none of the credit is needed that year.
Step 2: transfer. Up to $5,000 of this year's tuition can go to ONE person: a parent, a grandparent, or a spouse/common-law partner. That's $700 off their tax (5,000 × 14%), and only if they owe tax.
Step 3: carry forward. The other $3,000 stays with the student, with no expiry: a $420 credit, but only on the student's own future return. Carried-forward amounts can't be transferred later.

And the federal education and textbook amounts ended after 2016. Tuition is what's left.
