Same $100,000 of profit. Incorporate, and you keep about $6,000 more. But not from lower tax.

Option 1, sole proprietor (Manitoba 2026, single): income tax $21,729 + CPP $9,293 → you keep $68,978.
Option 2, a corporation paying it all out as dividends: 9% corporate tax ($9,000) + personal tax on $91,000 of dividends ($16,014) = $25,014 of income tax, $3,285 MORE than the sole proprietor. You keep $74,986.

Where does the $6,008 come from? Dividends skip CPP: no contributions, but no CPP pension built, and dividends create no RRSP room.

The real edge: money you leave inside. Leave $40,000 in the company and so far it has only been taxed at 9%; personal tax comes when you take it out (a deferral, not a discount).

A corporation also files its own tax return (T2) every year. Run your own numbers with an accountant first.
