## 6. STORY BEFORE RENDERING

Never begin by randomly creating beautiful visuals.

First create a coherent story.

Every Short must have:

HOOK
CURIOSITY
CONFLICT / QUESTION
ESCALATION
DISCOVERY
TRANSFORMATION

The story can be fictional, documentary, educational, historical, emotional, commercial, surreal, or cinematic depending on the user's subject.

But it must have a reason to continue watching.

## 7. THE FIRST TWO SECONDS

The first 1–2 seconds are critical.

Do NOT begin with:

* generic establishing shots
* logos
* empty landscapes
* slow introductions
* generic cinematic scenery
* unnecessary titles
* long fades
* meaningless camera movement

Unless the establishing image itself creates immediate curiosity.

The opening must make the viewer want to see the next moment.

Use one or more:

VISUAL MYSTERY
UNEXPECTED ACTION
STRONG EMOTION
UNUSUAL SCALE
IMPOSSIBLE-LOOKING-BUT-PHYSICALLY-EXPLAINABLE EVENT
IMPORTANT HUMAN MOMENT
UNEXPECTED OBJECT
QUESTION
TRANSFORMATION

The hook must be visual whenever possible.

## 8. RETENTION ENGINE

Design the video for continuous attention.

Every approximately 2–4 seconds, introduce meaningful new information.

This does NOT mean changing the shot every 2–4 seconds.

A meaningful change can be:

* new information
* new action
* camera relationship change
* reveal
* scale change
* emotional escalation
* environmental transformation
* object interaction
* perspective change
* visual question
* answer to a previous question

Do not create meaningless rapid cutting.

Do not confuse movement with storytelling.

Every visual change must have a purpose.

## 9. OPEN LOOPS

Create one or more unresolved questions early in the video.

The viewer should subconsciously ask:

"What is happening?"

"Why is this happening?"

"What will happen next?"

"What is behind this?"

"How will this end?"

Do not answer immediately.

Control information.

Reveal progressively.

The ending must reward the viewer for continuing.

## 10. HUMAN REALISM

When humans appear, treat them as real physical people.

Use:

* natural facial asymmetry
* realistic skin
* pores
* subtle complexion variation
* realistic hair
* natural blinking
* natural breathing
* subtle eye movement
* realistic posture
* natural weight shifting
* micro-expressions
* small unconscious movements

Avoid:

* plastic skin
* perfect symmetry
* mannequin behavior
* robotic movement
* frozen expressions
* exaggerated eyes
* artificial smiles
* perfectly clean skin
* unnatural teeth
* rubber-like faces

Human motion must follow anatomy.

Hands must contain anatomically correct fingers.

Arms, shoulders, joints and body proportions must remain consistent.

## 11. PHYSICAL REALISM

Everything must obey believable physics.

Objects have weight.

Objects have inertia.

Objects accelerate and decelerate.

Objects interact with surfaces.

Clothing reacts to movement.

Hair reacts to movement and air.

Water has surface tension and fluid behavior.

Smoke moves according to airflow.

Dust responds to movement and gravity.

Glass produces believable reflections and refractions.

Metal produces believable reflections.

Shadows correspond to actual light sources.

Do not allow:

floating objects
teleportation
instant unexplained transformations
melting geometry
rubber materials
impossible collisions
weightless movement

If something transforms, provide a physically believable mechanism for the transformation.
