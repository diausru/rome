VOICEOVER

Can you write off your car? Only the part you can prove. Say you drove twenty thousand kilometres this year. Twelve thousand were for your business. That's sixty percent business use. Gas, insurance, repairs, licence: six thousand two hundred dollars for the year. Sixty percent of that is three thousand, seven hundred and twenty. That's your deduction. In our sixty-thousand-dollar Manitoba example, that saves about thirteen hundred dollars of tax and C-P-P. But driving from home to your regular place of business? That's personal. And that seventy-three cents a kilometre? That's for employer allowances. Self-employed people claim actual costs. No logbook? The C-R-A can deny the claim. Date, destination, purpose, kilometres. Keep one full year. After that, a three-month sample can work, if your driving stays similar. Your car isn't the write-off. Your business kilometres are.

Description:
Self-employed in Canada? You can't write off your car. You can write off the business part of it, and only if you can prove it.

Example: 20,000 km driven, 12,000 for business = 60% business use. Gas, insurance, repairs and licence: $6,200. 60% × $6,200 = $3,720 deduction. In our 2026 Manitoba example ($60K net self-employment income) that saves about $1,323 of tax and CPP.

Two traps: driving from home to your regular place of business is personal, and the 73¢/km rate is the limit for employer-paid allowances; self-employed people claim actual costs. Keep a logbook (date, destination, purpose, km). After one full year, a 3-month sample can work if your driving stays similar.

Sources: CRA (canada.ca) motor vehicle expenses and simplified logbook; Department of Finance 2026 automobile rates. Example only; general information, not tax advice.
