Description:
Same receipts, same family: $116 on one return, $377 on the other.

The rule (federal, 2025): the medical expense credit only counts the part of your expenses above the lesser of 3% of your net income or $2,834. Expenses for you, your spouse or common-law partner and children under 18 (line 33099) can be claimed by either spouse.
• Alex, net income $90,000: 3% = $2,700 → $800 counts → $116.
• Sam, net income $30,000: 3% = $900 → $2,600 counts → $377.

The twist: the lower income usually wins, but the credit is non-refundable. It only cuts tax that spouse owes; it can't create a refund on its own. In this example Sam owes about $2,011 of federal tax before credits, so the $377 fits.

Run the claim on both returns before you choose.
