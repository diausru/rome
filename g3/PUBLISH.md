Description:
First paycheque smaller than you expected? Three deductions come off every pay in Canada: CPP, EI and income tax.

Example (Manitoba, 2026): $52,000 a year, paid every two weeks = $2,000 a cheque.
• CPP: about $110.99 (5.95% above the $3,500 yearly exemption)
• EI: about $32.60 (1.63% of insurable earnings, outside Quebec)
• Income tax: about $301.56 (federal + Manitoba, based on your TD1 forms)
→ take-home about $1,554.85.

The twist: once you reach the yearly maximum, CPP and EI deductions stop until January. For 2026 that's $85,000 of earnings for CPP (including the extra CPP2 between $74,600 and $85,000) and $68,900 for EI. At tax time, your return settles the difference: a refund or a balance owing.

The example uses yearly amounts ÷ 26; your employer's payroll tables can differ slightly. Sources: CRA payroll pages (canada.ca), 2026. General information, not advice.
