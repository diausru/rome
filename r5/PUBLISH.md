Description:
Move $40,000 of pension on paper, and this retired couple pays about $3,418 less tax a year. No money changes hands.

The example (Manitoba, 2026, both 66): Pat has $100,000 (an $80,000 workplace pension + $20,000 CPP/OAS). Jo has $20,000 (CPP/OAS).
The rule: Pat and Jo can jointly elect to report up to half of Pat's eligible pension income on Jo's return. $40,000 moves; both land at $60,000.
• Bracket tax: about $3,030 less ($40K leaves a 33.25% rate and lands mostly at 24.8%).
• Jo can now claim the pension credit: $388 more ($2,000 × 14% federal + $1,000 × 10.8% Manitoba).

What doesn't count: CPP and OAS can't be split this way. Under 65, RRIF withdrawals don't qualify; a life pension from a workplace plan does at any age (RRIF counts from 65).
The twist: no money moves. The cheque still goes to Pat. It's Form T1032, filed with both returns, every year.
