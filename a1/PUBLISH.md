FACT SOURCES
- Income Tax Act s.122.9 (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca, current to 2026-09-21) · 2026 · eligible educator school supply credit: refundable, 25% of up to $1,000 of eligible supplies; certified teacher or ECE at a school or regulated child care facility; not reimbursed
- Income Tax Act s.122.91 and s.118.5(1.2) · 2026 · Canada training credit: refundable, lesser of the training amount limit and 50% of eligible tuition; limit +$250 a year after 2019 if conditions are met (age 26–65, filed, resident, working income, income cap), lifetime $5,000; tuition credit reduced by 14% of the CTC
- canada.ca, Line 45350 Canada training credit · current · the limit appears on the latest notice of assessment or reassessment and in My Account
- Income Tax Act s.118.041 · 2026 · home accessibility tax credit: non-refundable, 14% of up to $20,000 of qualifying expenditures for a dwelling of a person 65+ or eligible for the DTC
- Income Tax Act ss.117(2)(a), 248(1) · 2026 · 14% lowest federal rate = credit rate

Case 1: she's a teacher and spent $1,000 of her own money on classroom supplies. The eligible educator school supply credit gives back 25%: $250. It's refundable.
Case 2: she's 40 and took a $3,000 course. Her Canada training credit limit grew $250 a year since 2020 (if she met the conditions every year: age 26–65, filed, working income). It covers half the fee: $1,500, also refundable. Her tuition credit is reduced a little in exchange.
Case 3: her mom, 70, added a walk-in shower and grab bars for $20,000. The home accessibility tax credit: 14% → $2,800 off her tax. It's non-refundable, so it only reduces tax she actually owes.

The twist: refundable credits pay you even if you owe no tax.
Check: your Canada training credit limit is on your notice of assessment and in CRA My Account. Keep every receipt.
