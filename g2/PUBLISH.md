Description:
Own an apartment back home or savings in a foreign bank? CRA may want Form T1135, even if you owe no tax.

Who files: Canadian residents whose specified foreign property cost more than $100,000 in total, at any time in the year. Under $250,000 all year → the simplified Part A; $250,000 or more → the detailed Part B.

Counts: money in foreign bank accounts, a rental property abroad, shares of foreign companies (U.S. stocks), even held at a Canadian broker.
Doesn't count: a vacation home you use mainly yourself, property used only in an active business, and anything inside an RRSP or TFSA.

Cost, not market value. For newcomers, the cost is the fair market value on the day you became resident.

The year you become resident: no T1135. The $100,000 test starts the next year. Your world income is still reported.

Late? The penalty is the greater of $100 or $25 a day, up to $2,500 (more if the failure is knowing). It's due with your return (April 30, or June 15 if you or your spouse run a business).

Sources: CRA, Foreign Income Verification Statement; Questions and answers about Form T1135; Questions and answers about penalties (canada.ca). The couple is an example. General information, not advice.
