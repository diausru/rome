Description:
Same daughter, same mom, same care. One year the caregiver credit is worth $1,247. The next, $0. The difference is mom's income.

The rule (federal, 2025 tax year, the return filed in spring 2026): you can claim the Canada caregiver amount for an infirm dependant 18 or older (line 30450): up to $8,601. It drops dollar for dollar once the dependant's net income is over $20,197, and is nil at $28,798.
• Mom's net income $18,000: full $8,601 × 14.5% = $1,247 off federal tax.
• $24,000: $8,601 − $3,803 = $4,798 × 14.5% = $696.
• $30,000, mostly OAS and CPP: $0.

The twist: the credit follows the dependant's income, not yours. If two people support the same parent, they can split it, but the total can't be more than one maximum.

Check the dependant's net income (line 23600) before you claim. The dependant must have an impairment and depend on you; a temporary illness doesn't count.
