### General information (rules)
| # | Claim used in the video | Source |
|---|---|---|
| R1 | Money given to relatives is not a deduction. The deductible "support payment" is support paid under a court order or written agreement to a current or former spouse / common-law partner (living apart because of a breakdown), or to the parent of your child. Parents are not a qualifying recipient | CRA "What are support payments" (…/support-payments/what-support-payments.html); "Definitions for support payments"; lines 21999 / 22000 |
| R2 | Canada caregiver amount for other infirm dependants 18+ (line 30450): the dependant can be your or your spouse's parent; they must have a physical or mental impairment; they must have been resident in Canada at any time in the year, unless they are your child or grandchild | CRA line 30450 page |
| R3 | Medical expenses for other dependants (line 33199): includes your or your spouse's parents who depended on you for support and were residents of Canada at any time in the year | CRA lines 33099 and 33199 page |
| R4 | Amount for an eligible dependant (line 30400): you were single, divorced, separated or widowed, and supported a dependant (a parent can qualify) who lived with you in a home you maintained; income limits apply. CRA may ask for proof that the parent depends on you | CRA line 30400 page |

### Eligibility requirements shown
Impairment (R2) · paid the expenses for a dependant (R3) · single and living with you (R4) · resident in Canada at some point in the year for a parent (R2, R3). The board footnote reads "Each credit has more conditions and income limits". The amounts and income limits are deliberately not quoted: 2026 values were found only in secondary sources ($8,773 / $20,601), and they aren't needed for the story.

### Exceptions
- Child or grandchild dependants are exempt from the Canadian-residence condition in R2 and R3. This does not apply to parents, so it is not shown.

### Dependencies (facts the topic can't establish)
- Whether a parent who comes to Canada becomes "resident" depends on their ties and stay. This is a factual and legal determination. The video says "Residency depends on the facts. Get advice."
- Eligible-dependant claims depend on marital status, living arrangement and income.

### Example (not a CRA rule)
"$500 a month" (= $6,000 a year) is a hypothetical labelled EXAMPLE on screen and in the footer.

### Claims deliberately NOT made
- That remittances are never relevant to anything (for example, other countries' rules).
- Any credit amount or threshold.
- That a written agreement with parents makes support deductible (one search summary implied this; CRA's definition of support recipients excludes parents).
