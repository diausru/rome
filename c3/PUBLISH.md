Description:
How does a small corporation in Manitoba pay just 9% tax on its profit? The small business deduction.

On the first $500,000 of active business income, a Canadian-controlled private corporation (CCPC) pays 9% federal tax, and Manitoba adds 0%. Above that, the general rate applies: 27% combined (15% federal + 12% Manitoba).

Example: $120,000 of profit → about $10,800 of corporate tax instead of $32,400.

Two traps: passive investment income over $50,000 inside the company shrinks the $500,000 limit ($5 for every $1, gone at $150,000), and an incorporated employee (a personal services business) doesn't get the deduction at all. Associated companies share one limit.

And it's a deferral, not a gift: when you pay yourself dividends, you pay personal tax on them.

Sources: CRA (canada.ca), Manitoba Finance (gov.mb.ca), 2026. The $120,000 company is an example. General information, not advice.
