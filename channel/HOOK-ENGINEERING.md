# VIRAL HOOK ENGINEERING — mandatory module (user, 2026-11-19)
Applies from the next video on command: Shorts (`/next-video`) and long-form (`/lf`). Read the APPLY block; the full user text below is reference.

## APPLY (how it works with our hard rules)
- **Order:** hook FIRST, then the script. "Start from the end": the opening shows the result, the surprising number or the reaction (the payoff), and the video then explains how we got there.
- **No AI video (hard rule stays):** a "visual generation prompt" = a Higgsfield still (`gpt_image_2_5`, 9:16 Shorts / 16:9 LF, no text/numbers) + motion in code (Remotion: DepthPhoto camera, kinetic numbers, handwriting, stamps, transforms). "Continuous shot" = one plate + a code camera move. Numbers appear only in code.
- **Channel priority (Tax Secrets Canada):** corrective hook (a CRA misconception), a surprising verified figure, a visual number demonstration, a silent writing action (a hand or pen writes the key number). Every claim in the hook must come from canada.ca/CRA with a tax year. Never exaggerate savings or promise refunds. No "secret/hack/loophole".
- **Stage 0 (before lines.py or the Short script), written into BLUEPRINT.md (LF) or `<p>/HOOK.md` (Shorts):**
  1. Concept · audience · the viewer problem, desire, misconception or curiosity.
  2. 5 different hook concepts, at least 3 of the 5 formats (A impossible shot · B reaction · C corrective · D pattern break · E silent action).
  3. A score table 1–5: first frame · instant comprehension · curiosity gap · audience relevance · emotion · link to the story · feasibility (stills + code) · credibility. Pick the best fit for the right audience, not the most dramatic.
  4. The chosen hook: a 0–0.5 / 0.5–1.5 / 1.5–3 / 3–4 s timeline (visual · on-screen text · VO · SFX · camera), the plate prompt, the camera move, the sound (one appropriate cue or silence, no loud stingers) and the exact transition into the story.
  5. Story chain: HOOK → CURIOSITY → PROBLEM → EXPLANATION → EVIDENCE → PAYOFF → CTA. Every promise the hook makes is paid off (note the line where).
- **The first frame is never** a logo, a greeting, an empty establishing shot or a slow move without purpose. Frame 0 = an event, a number or a reaction. It must work muted: on-screen text carries the hook.
- **QC gate (before render):** the 10 questions in §10 answered yes/no in BLUEPRINT/HOOK.md. If any answer is "no", rewrite the hook before the full render.
- **After publication only:** compare hooks with real analytics (3-s retention, AVD, % viewed, early drop-off, completion, shares/saves) via NexLev/vidIQ my-channel tools. Write them into `channel/HOOK-LOG.md`. Never invent numbers; unpublished videos have no data.
- **Budget:** the 5 concepts and the scores are text only (no extra generations). Only the chosen hook gets plates; it counts inside the existing 6 plates (LF) or the photo batch (Shorts).
- Out of scope here: THE PURE ESCAPE (another channel). Its section is kept below only as user reference.

## Full module (user text, 2026-11-19)
1. CORE: every short-form video is engineered with a powerful opening hook before the main video is produced. This is a mandatory stage. Goal: stop the scroll, create immediate curiosity and an emotional connection, and earn the next 5–10 s. Never begin with logos or animations, greetings or introductions, generic cinematic establishing shots, slow purposeless camera moves, empty branding, or explanations before curiosity. Start with an event, a visual surprise, a strong statement, a meaningful action or a compelling question.
2. FIVE FORMATS:
   - A Impossible shot: an unusual visual metaphor, a real object behaving unexpectedly, an impossible perspective, a transformation that communicates the subject.
   - B Reaction: a person already feeling surprise, disbelief, concern, realization or relief; show the reaction before its cause.
   - C Corrective: challenge a common mistake ("You're probably doing this wrong", "This tax mistake could cost you hundreds", "Most people misunderstand this CRA rule"); it must be specific, accurate and researched.
   - D Pattern break: an unexpected perspective, a sudden transformation, a surprising object, an unusual transition, a compelling action; it must relate to the subject, never unrelated spectacle.
   - E Silent action: an absorbing action without dialogue (a hand writing an important number, a document opened, a process demonstrated, an object transformed); it must be meaningful when muted.
3. SELECTION:
   1. Analyse the topic and audience.
   2. Find the key problem, desire, misconception or curiosity.
   3. Choose a format.
   4. Write 5 substantially different concepts.
   5. Evaluate relevance, originality, clarity, emotional impact and link to the story.
   6. Pick the strongest.
   7. Write a precise visual production prompt.
   8. Create the hook before assembling the main video.

   Don't automatically pick the most dramatic; pick what attracts the correct audience.
4. FIRST 3–4 s (guidelines, not rigid):
   - 0–0.5 s: immediate visual interruption.
   - 0.5–1.5 s: develop the curiosity; introduce the problem or the unexpected situation.
   - 1.5–3 s: the spoken hook, the reveal or the action.
   - 3–4 s: connect to the main story and give a reason to continue.
5. VISUAL SPEC per hook: aspect (9:16 Shorts), exact duration, camera position and angle, shot size and placement, lens, movement, character position and expression, lighting and environment, realistic movement, action timeline, SFX and ambience, dialogue if needed, exact transition. Realistic photographic look, no generic AI-ad look; believable environments, natural skin, plausible actions; a continuous shot when it helps realism, cuts when they help clarity.
6. AUDIO reinforces the visual: a sudden but appropriate sound, natural ambience, a recognizable physical sound, a short statement, strategic silence, a sound transition. No loud effects just for attention. Dialogue concise and synced. The visual hook must be understandable without audio.
7. CONNECTION: HOOK → CURIOSITY → PROBLEM → EXPLANATION → EVIDENCE → PAYOFF → CTA. Answer every question the hook raises. Explain a surprising visual. Correct and evidence a challenged misconception. Never make a promise the video doesn't fulfil.
8. CHANNELS:
   - Tax Secrets Canada: corrective hooks, surprising financial facts, visual number demos, CRA misconceptions, silent writing; real figures, verified law, CRA sources, accurate math; no exaggerated savings or guaranteed refunds.
   - The Pure Escape: pattern breaks, realistic massage demos, reactions, silent actions; correct anatomy and clinical settings; no medical promises or misleading before/after.
   - Educational / documentary: impossible metaphors, historical mysteries, discoveries, strong questions, visual evidence tied to the central question.
9. A/B: 5 alternative openings per important short. Score first-frame impact, comprehension, curiosity gap, relevance, emotion, story link, feasibility, credibility. After publication collect 3-s retention, AVD, % viewed, early drop-off, completion, rewatches, shares and saves. Compare them while accounting for topic, audience, distribution and timing. Never invent results.
10. FINAL QC:
    1. Would it stop the intended viewer?
    2. Is the first frame interesting?
    3. Is it understood instantly?
    4. Is there a clear reason to continue?
    5. Is it connected to the subject?
    6. Is it credible and achievable?
    7. Does the video deliver the promise?
    8. Does it work muted?
    9. Is it original?
    10. Is there a clear transition?

    If the opening is weak, rewrite it before producing.
11. OUTPUT per video:
    1. Concept.
    2. Audience.
    3. 5 hooks.
    4. The selected hook + why.
    5. The exact 3–4 s script.
    6. The visual prompt.
    7. Camera / movement.
    8. Audio.
    9. The transition.
    10. The full script.
    11. The retention strategy.
    12. The CTA.
    13. The post-publication metrics.

The hook is not a decorative intro; it is the first chapter of the story.
