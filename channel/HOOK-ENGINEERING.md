## Practical checklist for `/hw` (the module in 6 lines)
1. Before the script: 5 hook concepts (≥3 different formats from A–E), scored 1–5 on first frame, clarity, curiosity gap, audience fit, link to the story, feasibility, credibility. Pick one; write why.
2. Frame 0 already shows something: ink on the paper (pen moving, a number or a crossed-out wrong claim), never a blank sheet with the hand arriving. No logo, greeting or slow empty move.
3. 0–0.5 s interruption · 0.5–1.5 s the problem · 1.5–3 s the spoken hook / reveal · 3–4 s the bridge into the story. VO line 1 = the hook (≤12 words).
4. It works muted: the first written line alone says the hook.
5. The hook's promise is paid off on paper later (HOOK → CURIOSITY → PROBLEM → EXPLANATION → EVIDENCE → PAYOFF → CTA).
6. Final QC: the 10 questions of §10; if the opening is weak, rewrite it before the VO batch.

## Full module (as given by the user)

ADDITIONAL PRODUCTION MODULE: VIRAL HOOK ENGINEERING

### 1. CORE REQUIREMENT
Every short-form video must be engineered with a powerful opening hook before the main video is produced.
This is a mandatory production stage, not an optional creative enhancement.
The objective is to stop the viewer from scrolling, create immediate curiosity, establish an emotional connection, and earn the next 5–10 seconds of attention.

Never begin with:
* Channel logos or animations.
* Greetings or introductions.
* Generic cinematic establishing shots.
* Slow camera movements without a clear purpose.
* Empty branding sequences.
* Explanations before establishing curiosity.

Start with an event, visual surprise, strong statement, meaningful action, or compelling question.

### 2. FIVE HOOK FORMATS
Before writing any video script, evaluate all five formats:

**A. IMPOSSIBLE SHOT** — Create a visually unexpected or extraordinary scene that makes viewers stop and wonder what they are seeing. Examples: an unusual visual metaphor; a realistic object behaving unexpectedly; an impossible camera perspective; a visual transformation that communicates the subject.

**B. REACTION HOOK** — Start with a person already experiencing a recognizable emotion (surprise, disbelief, concern, realization, relief). Show the reaction before revealing its cause.

**C. CORRECTIVE HOOK** — Challenge a common mistake or misconception. Examples: "You're probably doing this wrong." "This tax mistake could cost you hundreds of dollars." "Deep tissue massage is not what most people think." "Most people misunderstand this CRA rule." The correction must be specific, accurate, and supported by research.

**D. PATTERN BREAK** — Break the viewer's visual expectations: an unexpected camera perspective; a sudden visual transformation; a surprising object entering the scene; an unusual transition between two environments; a visually compelling action that immediately raises a question. The visual must relate to the actual video subject. Never use unrelated spectacle simply to attract views.

**E. SILENT ACTION** — Start with an absorbing physical action without dialogue: a hand writing an important number; a document being opened; a massage therapist performing a specific technique; a visual demonstration of a process; an object being transformed. The action must communicate something meaningful even when the video is muted.

### 3. HOOK SELECTION SYSTEM
Before production:
1. Analyze the video topic and target audience.
2. Identify the most important viewer problem, desire, misconception, or curiosity.
3. Select the most appropriate hook format.
4. Generate five substantially different hook concepts.
5. Evaluate them for relevance, originality, clarity, emotional impact, and connection to the main story.
6. Select the strongest concept.
7. Write a precise visual production prompt.
8. Create the hook before assembling the main video.

Do not automatically choose the most dramatic concept. Choose the concept that best attracts the correct audience.

### 4. FIRST 3–4 SECONDS
Treat the opening as an independent production sequence.
- 0.0–0.5 s: Immediate visual interruption. Start with action, an unusual image, an expressive reaction, or a compelling statement.
- 0.5–1.5 s: Develop the visual curiosity. Introduce the central problem or unexpected situation.
- 1.5–3.0 s: Deliver the spoken hook, visual revelation, or meaningful action.
- 3.0–4.0 s: Connect the opening to the main story and create a reason to continue watching.

These are production guidelines, not rigid timing requirements. Adapt them to the subject and delivery.

### 5. VISUAL PRODUCTION REQUIREMENTS
For every generated hook, specify: aspect ratio 9:16; exact duration; camera position and angle; shot size and subject placement; lens perspective; camera movement; character positioning and expression; lighting and environment; realistic physical movement; detailed action timeline; sound effects and ambient audio; dialogue, if necessary; exact transition into the main video.

Use realistic photographic aesthetics. Avoid the generic AI-generated advertising look. Prefer believable environments, natural skin texture, realistic lighting, natural camera movement, and physically plausible actions. Use a continuous shot when it improves realism. Use cuts when they improve clarity or impact.

### 6. AUDIO AND SOUND DESIGN
Audio must reinforce the opening visual. Consider: a sudden but appropriate sound; natural environmental ambience; a recognizable physical sound; a short spoken statement; strategic silence; a sound transition into the main story.
Never use loud effects simply for attention. Dialogue must be concise, natural, and synchronized with the visual action when applicable. The visual hook must remain understandable without audio.

### 7. HOOK-TO-STORY CONNECTION
The hook must not be disconnected from the main content.
Sequence: HOOK → CURIOSITY → PROBLEM → EXPLANATION → EVIDENCE → PAYOFF → CTA.
If the hook raises a question, the main video must answer it. If the hook presents a surprising visual, explain its relevance. If the hook challenges a misconception, provide the correction and evidence. Never make a promise that the video does not fulfill.

### 8. CHANNEL-SPECIFIC APPLICATION
**TAX SECRETS CANADA:** Prioritize corrective hooks, surprising financial facts, visual number demonstrations, CRA-related misconceptions, and silent writing actions. Use real tax figures, verified Canadian legislation, CRA sources, and accurate calculations. Do not exaggerate tax savings or imply guaranteed refunds.

**THE PURE ESCAPE:** Prioritize pattern breaks, realistic massage demonstrations, reaction hooks, and visually absorbing silent actions. Show professional massage techniques, realistic therapist movements, correct anatomy, and appropriate clinical environments. Avoid medical promises, exaggerated treatment outcomes, and misleading before-and-after claims.

**YOUTUBE EDUCATIONAL AND DOCUMENTARY CHANNELS:** Prioritize impossible visual metaphors, historical mysteries, unexpected discoveries, strong questions, and visually compelling evidence. Connect the hook directly to the documentary's central question.

### 9. A/B TESTING AND PERFORMANCE ANALYSIS
For every important short-form video, develop five alternative opening concepts. Evaluate each using: first-frame visual impact; immediate comprehension; curiosity gap; audience relevance; emotional impact; connection to the main story; production feasibility; credibility.

After publication, collect available performance data: first 3-second retention, where available; average view duration; average percentage viewed; early audience drop-off; completion rate; rewatches; shares and saves.

Compare results between hooks while considering differences in topic, audience, distribution, and publication conditions. Use actual analytics to improve future hooks. Never invent performance results.

### 10. FINAL QUALITY CONTROL
Before approving any video, ask:
1. Would this opening make the intended viewer stop scrolling?
2. Is the first frame visually interesting?
3. Does the viewer understand what is happening?
4. Is there a clear reason to continue watching?
5. Does the opening connect to the actual subject?
6. Is the visual credible and technically achievable?
7. Does the video deliver on the hook's promise?
8. Can the opening work without sound?
9. Is the hook original rather than a copy of another creator?
10. Is there a clear transition into the main content?

If the opening is weak, rewrite it before producing the full video.

### 11. MANDATORY PRODUCTION OUTPUT
For every video, provide:
1. Main video concept.
2. Target audience.
3. Five alternative hook concepts.
4. Selected hook and justification.
5. Exact 3–4-second hook script.
6. Detailed visual generation prompt.
7. Camera and movement instructions.
8. Audio and sound design instructions.
9. Transition into the main video.
10. Complete main video script.
11. Retention strategy throughout the video.
12. CTA.
13. Performance metrics to evaluate after publication.

IMPORTANT: Do not treat the hook as a separate decorative introduction. The hook is the first chapter of the story and must be designed to make the viewer want to see what happens next.

This module is mandatory for all future short-form video production.
