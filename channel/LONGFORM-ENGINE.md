# TAX SECRETS CANADA — LONG-FORM YOUTUBE PRODUCTION SYSTEM (user, 2026-10-07)

Master command for 10–15 minute YouTube videos. Saved from the user's message of 2026-10-07; section numbers match the original. A long-form video is a separate editorial product, NOT a longer Short: deeper education, viewer trust, watch time, search and suggested discovery, returning viewers, authority, converting Shorts viewers. Duration ≈ 10–15 min, set by the strength and completeness of the story, never padded (a strong 10:20 beats a padded 15:00; a strong 14:40 beats a rushed 9:00).

1. **Core pipeline (closed loop):** RESEARCH → DISCOVER → ANALYZE → SELECT CONCEPT → VERIFY FACTS → DESIGN STORY → WRITE VOICEOVER → DESIGN VISUAL TIMELINE → GENERATE AUDIO → CREATE VIDEO → ASSEMBLE → QC → OPTIMIZE → CREATE PUBLISHING PACKAGE → WAIT FOR USER APPROVAL → OPTIONALLY SCHEDULE/PUBLISH THROUGH METRICOOL → ANALYZE PERFORMANCE → FEED RESULTS INTO FUTURE VIDEOS. The purpose is to continuously improve the channel, not just to make videos.
2. **Cadence:** about one long-form video every 10 days (2–3 per month). Quality, research and accuracy win over the calendar. Shorts continue on their own schedule; long-form and Shorts support each other.
3. **YouTube only:** no TikTok/Instagram packages in this mode unless asked. Design the video so Shorts can be extracted; the long-form video is the primary asset, Shorts are discovery assets.
4. **Phase one — market intelligence (before any writing):** what Canadians search; which tax questions gain attention; what viewers misunderstand; what is becoming relevant; what competitors publish; which videos outperform their channels; unusually strong engagement; titles that get clicks; thumbnails that create curiosity; repeated comment questions; evergreen vs seasonal subjects; subjects that spawn follow-ups.
5. **Use every research connector actually available** (vidIQ / vidIQ MCP, YouTube data and Analytics, Metricool, web/search, competitor tools, others). Inspect available tools first; never assume a connector exists; use the strongest source per task.
6. **vidIQ (hard cap ≤25 credits per video, user 2026-10-08; only the most important calls):** intelligence layer for Canadian tax / finance / competitor channels, breakout and outlier videos, titles, thumbnails, topics, views vs channel size, trends, audience interest, patterns. Study patterns, never copy creators; turn the opportunity into an original concept.
7. **Metricool:** analytics, competitor benchmarking, content performance, schedule, audience activity, historical trends. Its recommendations are not absolute truth; our own YouTube Analytics is the primary source for our performance.
8. **YouTube Analytics (when data exists):** impressions, CTR, views, watch time, AVD, average % viewed, retention, returning/new viewers, traffic sources, search terms, suggested, browse, subscribers gained (total and per video), likes, comments, shares, top moments, spikes, dips. Compare long-form only against long-form, never against Shorts.
9. **Outliers:** prefer videos that significantly outperform their channel's norm (views, CTR, retention, subscriber conversion, comments, shares, search traffic). Ask WHY it worked; copy the mechanism's lesson, not the surface.
10. **Keyword and search intent:** primary keyword, secondary keywords, long-tail terms, related questions, search intent, information gap, viewer pain point, curiosity angle, competition level, evergreen value, current value. Use keywords naturally, no stuffing; tags only for spelling variants. Priority: TOPIC + TITLE + THUMBNAIL + VIEWER SATISFACTION + RETENTION.
11. **Topic selection:** generate 10 candidate topics, score each 1–10 on search demand, viewer pain, curiosity, evergreen, current relevance, competition opportunity, visual potential, story potential, monetization relevance, Shorts potential. Pick the best combination of DEMAND + CURIOSITY + STORY + VALUE + DIFFERENTIATION, not simply the highest volume. (When the user commands a specific queue topic, this step sharpens the angle of that topic rather than replacing it.)
12. **Concept document before the script:** title concept; one-sentence promise; target viewer; main question; main conflict; information gap; central reveal; emotional arc; final payoff; why this video should exist; why watch it instead of another. Weak concept → fix it before continuing.
13. **Story engine (framework, adapt to the story):** 0:00–0:30 hook · 0:30–1:30 promise + setup · 1:30–3:00 first discovery · 3:00–5:00 deeper explanation · 5:00–7:00 complication / surprise · 7:00–9:00 real numbers / case study · 9:00–11:00 second scenario / comparison · 11:00–13:00 practical application · 13:00–15:00 final answer + takeaway.
14. **No long intro:** no "Welcome back to Tax Secrets Canada", no 20 s about who we are. Start with the viewer's problem and explain immediately.
15. **Open loop** in the first 10–20 s that is genuinely answered later. No fake suspense.
16. **Chapters** that each accomplish something and help comprehension (e.g. 01 The $100K question · 02 Federal tax · 03 Provincial tax · 04 CPP and EI · 05 The surprise · 06 Real-life scenario · 07 What changes the result · 08 The final number).
17. **Information density:** remove repetition, generic statements, intros, filler, excessive transitions, motivational talk. Test every section: "Does this change what the viewer understands?" If not, cut it.
18. **Explain simply:** assume no knowledge of marginal rates, taxable income, deductions, credits, CPP, EI, withholding, brackets, capital gains, RRSP mechanics, provincial systems. Concept first → math → practical consequence.
19. **Real numbers:** concrete examples (e.g. $80K / $100K / $150K) shown as gross → taxable income → federal tax → provincial tax → CPP → EI → credits/deductions → estimated take-home. Never simplify into something misleading.
20. **Fact-checking:** CRA, Government of Canada, provincial governments, Statistics Canada; current tax year. When sources conflict, find out why (tax year, federal vs provincial, employee vs self-employed, individual vs corporation, gross vs taxable, credit vs deduction, maximum vs actual). Never silently pick a number.
21. **Traceability** in the project bible for every major figure: CLAIM · SOURCE · TAX YEAR · DATE CHECKED · CALCULATION METHOD.
22. **Voiceover:** professional Canadian financial education — authoritative, calm, intelligent, conversational, human, clear, slightly cinematic. No robotic narration, hype, YouTuber clichés, filler or repeated phrases. The VO is the spine.
23. **Audio first:** complete VO before video — wording, duration, pauses, emphasis, pronunciation, numbers, transitions, chapter timing. Generate it with the connected workflow (series voice: Grady via Higgsfield, see CLAUDE.md); otherwise deliver an exact audio spec. Audio is locked to the master timeline.
24. **One master timeline:** every important visual lands with its narration (e.g. 00:42 "Now here's where provincial tax changes the picture" → province comparison starts; 00:48 "Alberta…" → Alberta card; 00:53 "…versus Manitoba" → Manitoba card).
25. **Claude makes the actual video** (not a storyboard), choosing the right tech: Remotion, Canvas, SVG, WebGL, Three.js, FFmpeg, Python, procedural animation, shaders, compositing, etc.
26. **Visual language (pick per section, not all at once):** cinematic financial cards (premium glass/soft panels); realistic handwritten explanation; realistic documents (paperwork, receipts, notices); cinematic everyday Canadian scenarios; simple data visualization; large legible numerical animation.
27. **Visual change:** never one screen for minutes; every change (new calculation, camera move, example, chart, document, handwritten note, comparison, reveal, metaphor, scenario) must serve comprehension or retention.
28. **Thumbnails before the final title:** ≥3 concepts, each with one dominant visual, minimal text, strong contrast, clear subject, curiosity, instant mobile readability; no clutter, no paragraphs, no repeating the title. Title + thumbnail = one curiosity package.
29. **Titles:** 5 concepts scored on clarity, curiosity, search relevance, emotional tension, accuracy, differentiation; pick the strongest; no misleading clickbait.
30. **First-30-seconds QC before final render:** thumbnail promise delivered? topic instantly clear? compelling question? no unnecessary intro? not too slow? would a new viewer know why to continue?
31. **Retention design:** every 30–90 s a real reason to continue (new question, calculation, unexpected result, comparison, scenario, correction of a common belief, important number, reveal). No artificial cliffhangers.
32. **Top-moment engine:** in post, mark the strongest moments as future Shorts, long-form topics, hooks, thumbnail concepts, follow-ups.
33. **Render:** final MP4, 16:9, 1920×1080 master, 24 or 30 fps per the visuals, ≈10–15 min, YouTube-appropriate codec/container (H.264 + AAC in MP4).
34. **Self-QC on the actual file:** VIDEO — no broken/black frames, artifacts, glitches, broken text, discontinuities. AUDIO — no clipping, sudden level changes, timing errors; voice intelligible; music under the voice. STORY — no dead sections or repetition; payoff and promise delivered. FACTS — numbers, calculations, tax year, sources correct. VISUALS — support the narration; no needless animation; no generic AI look.
35. **Re-render rule:** a serious problem is fixed at the root, re-rendered and re-inspected until it passes; never just reported.
36. **Final content package (after the video is approved):** YouTube title, description, chapters, hashtags, pinned comment, CTA, thumbnail concept, thumbnail text, primary keyword, secondary keywords, search questions, sources, disclaimer where appropriate. No keyword-overloaded description.
37. **Long-form → Shorts:** 3–7 candidates, each with title idea, hook, source timecode, why it works, key visual, payoff, CTA to the long-form. Re-edited as standalone Shorts, not random 60 s cuts.
38. **Content ecosystem per video:** 3 next long-form topics, 3–7 Shorts, 3 follow-up questions, 3 community questions, 1–2 series concepts.
39. **Metricool publication only after approval:** video complete → QC complete → USER APPROVAL → prepare Metricool post → USER CONFIRMS → schedule. If Metricool is not connected, give the exact manual upload package.
40. **Never publish or schedule a public video without explicit user approval.** Research, drafting, rendering, optimizing and preparing Metricool data are allowed.
41. **Post-publication analysis** (when data exists): CTR, impressions, AVD, % viewed, retention, top moments, spikes, dips, traffic sources, subscribers, comments, likes, shares, search terms — vs previous long-form, expectations, similar topics, competitor benchmarks.
42. **Learning loop:** after every long-form video update the Production Bible (`longform/LONGFORM-BIBLE.md`): what worked, what failed, best hook, title, thumbnail style, retention moment, visual style, topic, search intent, CTA, video length, audience response, comments/questions, next opportunity.

44. **Absolute creative rule:** not "make a 15-minute video" but RESEARCH → FIND AN OPPORTUNITY → SELECT THE STRONGEST QUESTION → BUILD A STORY → PROVE IT WITH REAL NUMBERS → EXPLAIN IT SIMPLY → VISUALIZE IT → KEEP THE VIEWER CURIOUS → DELIVER THE PAYOFF → VERIFY EVERYTHING → CREATE THE FINAL VIDEO → CREATE THE NEXT CONTENT FROM IT → MEASURE → LEARN → IMPROVE THE NEXT VIDEO. The goal is a recognizable, authoritative Canadian tax channel, not video quantity.
45. **Positioning:** "The place Canadians go when they want someone to explain the tax system in plain English — with real numbers." Trustworthy, visually premium, simple, evidence-based, Canadian, practical, understandable, entertaining without sensationalism. Never trade accuracy for clicks, understanding for complexity, or originality for trends.
46. **Pillars:** 1 Real tax math ($100K salary; $80K vs $100K vs $150K; federal + provincial; CPP + EI; take-home; brackets). 2 Canadian tax explained (brackets, deductions, credits, RRSP, TFSA, capital gains, employment and side income, refunds, CRA processes). 3 Province vs province (AB vs MB, ON vs AB, BC vs AB, MB vs SK; same salary, different province). Use current numbers and state assumptions.
47. **Final standard:** at the end the viewer can answer — Why should I care? What did I learn? What surprised me? What number matters? What should I do with this information? If not, the video is not finished.
