MASTER PRODUCTION PIPELINE — SHORTS 9:16

This is an additional production mode inside the TAX SECRETS CANADA system.

Use this mode when the subject is better explained through a realistic human hand physically writing calculations, formulas, keywords, arrows, diagrams, comparisons or tax concepts on paper.

The final result must look like a professionally filmed real human hand explaining financial information on paper — NOT like an AI animation, slideshow, whiteboard animation template, or motion-graphics presentation.

The final output must be a complete 45–60 second vertical YouTube Short / TikTok / Instagram Reel.

## 0. CORE CREATIVE PRINCIPLE

The viewer must feel:

"I am watching a real person explain this concept by writing it down in front of me."

The illusion must be photographic and physical.

Prioritize:

1. Physical realism
2. Hand realism
3. Pen-to-paper interaction
4. Natural writing movement
5. Accurate information
6. Voiceover synchronization
7. Cinematography
8. Retention
9. Visual beauty
10. Technical complexity

Never sacrifice realism for visual effects.

## 6. VOICEOVER TIMING

Create exact timecodes.

Example:

[00:00.00–00:03.20]
"You make one hundred thousand dollars…"

[00:03.20–00:06.40]

Every spoken section must fit naturally inside the actual video.

Calculate realistic speaking speed.

Allow natural pauses.

Important numbers must have enough time to be heard AND visually understood.

If the voiceover does not fit:

DO NOT simply shorten the narration without considering the story.

Adjust the visual timeline and rerender.

## 9. PHOTOREALISTIC HAND

The hand is one of the most important elements.

It must appear like a real adult human hand.

Maintain continuity throughout the entire video:

* same skin tone
* same hand proportions
* same fingers
* same fingernails
* same wrist
* same sleeve
* same cuff
* same pen
* same grip

Never change the hand between shots.

Avoid:

* distorted fingers
* extra fingers
* missing fingers
* rubber-like skin
* plastic skin
* floating hand
* unnatural wrist bends
* impossible joints
* teleporting pen
* changing sleeve
* changing pen geometry

The hand must interact physically with the paper.

## 10. PEN PHYSICS

The pen must remain physically attached to the hand.

The pen tip must visibly contact the paper whenever writing occurs.

Maintain:

* consistent pen orientation
* realistic grip
* realistic finger pressure
* realistic wrist rotation
* realistic acceleration/deceleration

The pen must never write while hovering above the paper.

The ink must originate exactly from the pen tip.

## 11. REALISTIC HANDWRITING ENGINE

NEVER reveal handwritten text using:

* fade-in
* opacity animation
* slide-in
* scale animation
* random text appearance
* typewriter text effect

Instead, handwriting must be constructed from actual drawing strokes.

Preferred technical approaches may include:

* SVG paths
* Canvas paths
* vector stroke animation
* procedural path drawing
* WebGL when useful
* masks
* path-tracing
* rasterized stroke animation
* custom procedural drawing systems

Choose the technically strongest method available.

Do not lock yourself to one framework.

The final effect must be:

PEN TIP MOVES
→ INK APPEARS UNDER PEN
→ LETTER FORMS
→ HAND MOVES TO NEXT STROKE
→ WORD COMPLETES

## 12. HUMAN WRITING IMPERFECTION

Do not make the handwriting mathematically perfect.

Introduce subtle natural variation:

* slight stroke-speed variation
* tiny pauses
* slight acceleration
* natural corners
* minor pressure variation
* tiny line irregularities
* realistic spacing
* natural hand repositioning

Do NOT make the imperfections excessive.

The goal is:

REALISTIC HUMAN WRITING

not:

MESSY HANDWRITING.

## 13. STROKE ORDER

Handwriting must follow a believable stroke order.

For each character:

1. determine natural stroke order
2. determine stroke direction
3. determine stroke speed
4. determine hand path
5. animate the hand along that path
6. reveal ink progressively along the same path

The hand and ink must never disagree.

If the hand moves left while the ink appears on the right, the illusion fails.

## 14. ARROWS, CIRCLES AND UNDERLINES

Arrows, circles, brackets and underlines must also be physically drawn.

Example:

The hand writes:

FEDERAL TAX

Then:

* moves to the right
* draws the arrow
* points toward the relevant number

Do NOT create the arrow with a graphic animation.

It must be drawn by the pen.

Circles should not be mathematically perfect.

They should look like a human quickly circled something important.

## 15. CALCULATIONS

## Implementation notes (Claude, 2026-10-06; not part of the user's text)

- **Reference** (`channel/refs/handwritten-reference.png`, 941×1672): overhead ~70° angle; warm low sun from the upper left casting plant-leaf shadows across the paper; walnut/oak desk; a blank sheet centred; a right hand entering from the lower right with a white shirt cuff and a dark suit sleeve; a black fine-liner held in a tripod grip; props: a small paper Canadian flag, an espresso mug on a cork coaster, a calculator, a leather notebook with a gold pen. Used as a STYLE reference only; our scene is original.
- **Known limitation, from TAX CASE #001 (bible D7):** a fully animated CG hand from the assets reachable in this container rendered like a mannequin. For this mode the planned path is a **photographic hand plate** (a still image of a real-looking hand and pen, matted) moved by code along the actual stroke paths: wrist translation plus small rotation, a pen tip locked to the ink head, a contact shadow, and ink revealed progressively under the tip from vector stroke paths with stroke-order data. This is standard tabletop practice and keeps the fingers anatomically intact because they are never deformed. Finger flexion is not simulated; this is a disclosed limitation.
- Still plates follow bible D2: stills may be used as static sources only; all motion, ink, camera and light are code. No external video-generation models (Master preamble).
- The voiceover follows `channel/VOICEOVER-ENGINE.md` (the repo's mandatory rule). §20/§21 of this mode also allow the user to record audio separately; both the timecoded script and a synthesized, synced track are delivered.
