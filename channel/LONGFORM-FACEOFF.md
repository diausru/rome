# TAX SECRETS CANADA — LONG-FORM FACE-OFF VIDEO SYSTEM (user, 2026-10-08)

Core visual and storytelling rule for ALL 10–15 minute videos. Used whenever the topic involves comparison, choice, money, software, provinces, strategies, tax situations or competing options. Section numbers match the user's text. Not a copy of any channel: a simple, recognizable, affordable TAX SECRETS CANADA language that still looks premium and cinematic.

1. **One visual world per episode** (and across the channel): NO permanent talking head. One cinematic desk/environment, laptop or monitor, tax documents, calculator, Canadian financial objects, subtle Canadian identity, glass/frosted UI panels, simple premium icons, clean numbers, restrained motion graphics, cinematic lighting, realistic materials, subtle camera movement. Recognizable from the first second to the last. No new environment every 20 s, no new AI character per section, no filler stock footage, no cinematic scenes that don't advance the story. Feeling: "This is the TAX SECRETS CANADA universe."
2. **Face-off is the story engine:** QUESTION → CONTENDERS → RULES → TEST → ROUND 1 → ROUND 2 → ROUND 3 → SURPRISE → SCORE → FINAL FACE-OFF → VERDICT (not intro → explanation ×3 → conclusion). The viewer always wants to know "Which one wins?"; the answer is not revealed early; a genuine information gap (e.g. "Three tax programs can all file your return. Which is best for a Canadian making $100,000? The cheapest is NOT necessarily the winner.") and then the contenders immediately.
3. **First 5 seconds:** never "Hi everyone / welcome back", no channel intro, no generic explanation of Canadian taxes. Start with the conflict: the contenders on screen at once, big central question (WHICH ONE SHOULD YOU USE?), a numerical contrast (e.g. $0 VS $30+ VS $90+ — only verified figures), "Same tax return. Very different experience." → "Which one actually wins?" The first visual communicates the whole conflict without knowing the channel.
4. **12-act visual structure** (≈10–14 acts, adapt to the story): 1 Hook 0:00–0:20 (contenders, question, numeric contrast, open loop, no winner) · 2 The rules 0:20–1:00 (3–5 criteria with minimal icons, e.g. PRICE / EASE / FEATURES / COMPLEXITY / VALUE) · 3 Trust / eligibility 1:00–1:45 (CRA / NETFILE / regulatory context; documents and simple verification graphics; uncluttered) · 4 The contenders 1:45–2:30 (all together: name, simple identifier, one-line positioning) · 5 Round one 2:30–4:00 (interface, UX, price, strengths, weaknesses, ideal user, told as a mini-story "If you are this type of Canadian, this is what happens") · 6 Round two 4:00–5:30 and 7 Round three 5:30–7:00 (identical framing, only the competitor changes) · 8 Score battle 7:00–8:30 (all back, one evolving scoreboard, no final winner yet) · 9 Which one for you? 8:30–10:00 (employee / family / self-employed / investor / student / newcomer, one icon each, the fitting option per person) · 10 Hidden costs / mistakes 10:00–11:30 (second curiosity spike: extra fees, upgrades, missing features, unneeded services, complexity, wrong assumptions) · 11 Final face-off 11:30–13:30 (top contenders, "FINAL FACE-OFF", ranking becomes clear, never a new competitor here) · 12 Verdict 13:30–15:00 (best overall / value / complex returns / families / simple returns; answer the opening question; bridge to the next video).
5. **Consistent visual language:** dark cinematic background, warm desk light, glass panels, white type, warm gold for key numbers, restrained green/red accents, subtle Canadian red, soft reflections, realistic shadows, premium financial-documentary look. Avoid cartoon graphics, childish icons, neon, random gradients, giant logos, emojis, corporate stock imagery, clutter. Feel: modern, Canadian, financial, premium, simple, trustworthy.
6. **One icon family** (price = coins/dollar, ease = hand/cursor, features = gear, tax = document, employee = briefcase, family = people, investor = chart, self-employed = laptop, CRA = government/document): same stroke weight, perspective, treatment, material and animation language. It becomes part of the channel identity.
7. **Reusable asset library, built once, reused every episode:** brand marker, Canadian flag, tax document template, calculator, laptop, desk environment, glass card, comparison card, score card, ranking podium, price badge, checkmark, warning icon, arrow, number counter, province card, employee / family / investor / self-employed icons.
8. **Cheapest appropriate method:** ≈60–80 % procedural (HTML / SVG / Canvas / WebGL / Remotion / FFmpeg / compositing), ≈20–40 % realistic generated visuals only where they improve the story (desk, laptop, tax document, Canadian home, person receiving a paycheque). Graphics for numbers, realistic visuals for situations. (Channel rule stays: no video-generation model; generated stills + code motion.)
9. **One human only if it adds value**, and then the same person, clothing, look, environment, lighting and camera language all episode. No changing faces, no random people for variety.
10. **Retention = information change**, not visual chaos: every ≈15–30 s a new number, competitor, comparison, question, score change, scenario, reveal, camera push, ranking move or unexpected result. The screen need not change completely; the information must.
11. **Evolving face-off score** on meaningful criteria (PRICE / EASE / FEATURES / COMPLEX TAX / SUPPORT / VALUE), not stars alone; the winner is discovered, not announced.
12. **No fake winner:** research current products, verify current pricing, verify CRA / NETFILE status, verify current features, test available information, compare real use cases, list strengths and weaknesses, then decide on evidence. Data changes → ranking changes. No sponsor favouritism; sponsorship disclosed, editorial independence kept.
13. **Sponsors inside the format:** no story-interrupting "today's video is sponsored by…" beyond the required disclosure; a sponsor is evaluated with exactly the same criteria as every competitor. Trust beats a short-term placement.
14. **Audio + visual sync:** complete VO, exact timecodes, visual events, on-screen text, animation timing, transitions, emphasis points. Every important spoken number has its visual at the moment it is spoken (e.g. "thirty dollars here, ninety there" → $30 → $90).
15. **Cost control:** no hundreds of unique AI clips, no new environment per shot, no expensive generation for simple graphics. Reuse desk, laptop, calculator, documents, cards, icons, UI components, transitions, animation systems; change data, text, numbers, competitor, scenario, ranking.
16. **Acts are not static slides:** each act has ≈2–6 meaningful shots / animation states (e.g. laptop appears → camera closer → interface → price → highlights → negative point → score changes → transition).
17. **Face-off loop:** WHO WINS? → HERE'S THE TEST → A → B → C → THE SCORE CHANGES → BUT THERE'S A PROBLEM → NEW TEST → THE RANKING CHANGES → FINAL FACE-OFF → VERDICT.
18. **Thumbnail = first frame:** the first 1–2 s show the same contenders as the thumbnail ("I clicked the right video").
19. **Visual economy test per scene:** does it explain, create curiosity, prove, compare, create emotional relevance or reveal? None → cut it. No visuals just for length.
20. **Final standard:** a premium Canadian financial investigation through a simple visual system — not a slideshow, stock compilation, AI movie, talking-head podcast or corporate explainer. CLEAR STORY + FACE-OFF + REAL NUMBERS + SIMPLE VISUALS + CINEMATIC PRESENTATION + STRONG RETENTION + LOWER COST + CONSISTENT IDENTITY. The viewer always knows: who is competing, what we test, who is winning, what changed, why it matters to me, and finally who wins.

**Claude's responsibility:** the whole production, end to end — research → verify → select the story → face-off structure → VO → 10–15 min timeline → create/reuse assets → animations → necessary generated visuals → assemble → sync audio → render MP4 → watch/inspect → fix → re-render → final QC → YouTube publishing package. Never stop at a script, prompts or a storyboard; the objective is the finished video.

## Applying it to non-product topics (Claude's working note, to confirm per episode)
The contenders change with the topic: incomes ($80K vs $100K vs $150K), provinces, strategies (RRSP vs TFSA), scenarios (one job vs two jobs). For an explainer such as LF-01 "Tax Brackets" the face-off can be e.g. three Canadians at different incomes or "what people think happens vs what actually happens"; the angle is chosen after research and stays different from the Shorts.
