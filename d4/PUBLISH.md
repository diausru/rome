Description:
One spouse earns $120,000, the other $30,000. Same $10,000 RRSP deposit. Whose name it's in changes the tax.

Alex puts $10,000 into a spousal RRSP (Sam owns it). Alex claims the deduction, from Alex's own RRSP room, and saves about $3,953 (Manitoba 2026, 43.4% bracket).
Years later, Sam withdraws the $10,000. At Sam's lower income, the tax at today's rates is about $2,480 (24.8%). Roughly $1,500 less.

The trap: if Sam withdraws while Alex contributed to a spousal RRSP this year or in the two years before, the withdrawal is taxed to Alex: about $4,340 (ITA 146(8.3)).
The twist: the rule counts calendar years. One deposit in December 2026 keeps 2026, 2027 and 2028 locked; 2029 is clear.

Time withdrawals around the last contribution, and check with an accountant before taking money out.
