Primary Title: Paying for Daycare? Which Parent Claims It (Canada)

Alternative Title B: Daycare Tax Deduction in Canada: Up to $8,000 per Child

Description:
Paying for daycare? Part of it can come off your taxable income, but usually only one parent can claim it.

What can count: daycare centres, nursery schools, caregivers like babysitters or nannies, and day camps. Yearly limit per child: $8,000 under age 7, $5,000 from 7 to 16 ($11,000 if the child is eligible for the disability tax credit).

Generally, the parent with the lower net income claims it, and the claim is capped at two-thirds of that parent's earned income. Example: parents earning $90,000 and $30,000, $9,000 of daycare for a 3-year-old → the $30,000 parent claims $8,000.

The higher earner claims only in specific cases (e.g. the other parent was in school or in hospital), with weekly limits on Form T778. Keep every receipt; for a babysitter, their SIN must be on it. And because it lowers net income, it can help your Canada Child Benefit too.

Sources: CRA, canada.ca (line 21400, Form T778, Folio S1-F3-C1). The family is an example. General information, not advice.
