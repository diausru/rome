Description:
Renting out a property in Canada? Here's what you can deduct, and the one deduction that can come back when you sell.

Current expenses are deducted the same year: mortgage interest (not the principal), property tax, insurance, repairs, utilities, management fees. Your own labour isn't deductible.

Repair or improvement? Fixing a wooden step restores it: a current expense. Replacing it with concrete improves it: a capital cost.

Capital costs of the building are claimed slowly as capital cost allowance (CCA): most rental buildings are Class 1, 4% a year on the declining balance, half that in the year you buy. Never on the land. CCA can't create or increase a rental loss, and it's optional: claim any amount, or none.

The trap: example: a $300,000 building, full CCA for 10 years = $96,395 deducted. Sell the building for more than you paid, and that $96,395 comes back as recaptured CCA: added to your income in the year of sale and fully taxable (a capital gain is only half taxable).

CCA is often a deferral, not a gift. Plan before you claim.

Sources: CRA guide T4036 Rental Income; CCA for rental property; Form T776 (canada.ca). The $300,000 building is an example. General information, not advice.
