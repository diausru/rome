## A. Final script
Leaving Canada for good? CRA can tax your investments, even if you never sell them.
When you become a non-resident, most of what you own is treated as sold at market value. That's the departure tax.
Say you bought shares for forty thousand. They're worth a hundred thousand when you go. That's a sixty-thousand-dollar gain, and half of it, thirty thousand, is added to your income.
Not included: your RRSP, TFSA and pensions, and personal items under ten thousand dollars.
Lived here five years or less? Property you brought with you is generally out.
If everything you own is worth more than twenty-five thousand, you also list it on form T1161.
Can't pay now? Form T1244 defers it, interest-free, until you sell. Owe over sixteen thousand five hundred federally? CRA wants security.
Next: your tuition slip, and what it's worth at tax time. Follow so you don't miss it.
