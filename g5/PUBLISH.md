Description:
Leaving Canada for good? CRA can tax your investments even if you never sell them. This is the departure tax (often called the exit tax).

The rule: when you become a non-resident, most property is treated as sold at fair market value on that day (and bought back at the same value). The gain is reported on Form T1243.

Example: shares outside an RRSP or TFSA bought for $40,000 and worth $100,000 when you leave → a $60,000 gain, and half of it, $30,000, is added to your income that year.

Not treated as sold: RRSPs, RRIFs, TFSAs and pension plans, and personal-use items worth under $10,000. Resident 60 months or less in the last 10 years? Property you owned when you arrived (or inherited after) is generally excluded, unless it's taxable Canadian property.

Paperwork: if everything you own is worth more than $25,000, list it on Form T1161. Can't pay? Form T1244 defers the tax, interest-free, until you sell; elect by April 30 of the year after you leave. Federal tax over $16,500 → CRA requires security.

When you become a non-resident depends on your ties to Canada. Plan with a professional before you go.
