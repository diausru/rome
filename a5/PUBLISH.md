Description:
A CRA notice says you owe $5,000. Before you pay, or panic, check three usual suspects.

Suspect 1: a forgotten slip. CRA matches slips (T4, T5…). Example: $15,000 of T5 interest never reported, taxed at about 33.25% (20.5% federal + 12.75% Manitoba at an ~$80K income) = $4,988, nearly the whole bill. CRA can generally reassess within 3 years of the original notice.
Suspect 2: a payment applied to another year. Your CRA My Account shows how each payment was applied.
Suspect 3: instalments. If your net tax owing was over $3,000 this year and in one of the two previous years, CRA expected payments during the year, and charges interest on late ones.

The twist: you have time. CRA can't start legal collection for 90 days after the notice, and you can object until the later of 90 days after the notice or one year after the filing deadline. But interest compounds daily from April 30, even while you sort it out.

Action: open the notice, read the explanation of changes, match every slip in My Account, then pay or object before the deadline.
