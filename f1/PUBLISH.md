Description:
Sold a stock for a profit? In Canada you're not taxed on all of it: only half.

Your capital gain = selling price − your cost (the adjusted cost base) − selling costs. Example: bought shares for $10,000, sold for $16,000, $50 in fees → a $5,950 gain. Only half, $2,975, is added to your income and taxed at your regular rate.

The proposed increase of the inclusion rate to two-thirds was cancelled: it's still one-half.

Capital losses only offset capital gains; unused net capital losses can be carried back 3 years or forward indefinitely. One trap: sell at a loss and buy the same shares back within 30 days (before or after), and the loss is denied (superficial loss). Inside a TFSA, gains are generally not taxed.

Track your cost base: every sale goes on your return (Schedule 3).

Sources: CRA guide T4037; Department of Finance (canada.ca). The share sale is an example. General information, not advice.
