You wrote off the whole laptop. Then you sold it for $700. Here's the catch.

Example (continuing the last video): a $2,000 laptop claimed 100% as capital cost allowance (Class 50). Its tax value (undepreciated capital cost) is now $0.
• Sell it for $700: $0 − $700 = −$700. A negative balance at year end is a recapture of CCA, and it goes back into business income (Form T2125, line 8230).
• For a Manitoba freelancer netting $60,000, that's about $249 in tax + CPP (2026 rates).
• Fair, really: you deducted value the laptop never actually lost.

The fix: buy the replacement in the same year. Its cost goes into the class first: $0 + $2,500 − $700 = $1,800 still to claim, and no recapture.
