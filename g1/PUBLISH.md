Description:
New to Canada? Your first tax return works differently. Here's what changes in year one.

1. Residency: you become a resident for tax purposes when you establish significant residential ties (a home, a spouse or partner, social ties), usually the day you arrive. Unsure? Form NR74.
2. World income: from that day you report income from every country (a tax treaty can exempt some). Foreign income earned before you arrived is generally not taxed in Canada.
3. Statement of income: you still list what you earned abroad, up to two years before, so CRA can calculate your benefits.
4. Prorated credits: most federal credits are prorated by days resident. Example: arrive September 1, 2026 → 122 days → basic personal amount ≈ $5,499 instead of $16,452.

Benefits: apply right away, before your first return: Form RC151 (GST/HST credit, now the Canada Groceries and Essentials Benefit) or RC66 if you have children. You need a SIN first.

Then file by April 30, every year, even with no income, or the payments stop.

Sources: CRA, Newcomers to Canada; Completing your return for newcomers; Federal non-refundable tax credits for newcomers; RC151; RC66 (canada.ca). The September example is illustrative. General information, not advice.
